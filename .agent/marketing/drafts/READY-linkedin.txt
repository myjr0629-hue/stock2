Your stock app is probably mixing two different sessions in one row.
You see a price with a percentage next to it. The price may be a pre-market print while the percentage is measured from yesterday's regular-session close. Two different sessions, one row.
Why it happens: market data APIs return the regular session and extended hours as separate fields. It is easy to render the newest price and reuse the percentage that was already wired up. The row looks correct. The arrow points the right way. The base is wrong.
Why it matters: earnings come out after the close, so the first move you ever see on an earnings reaction is an extended-hours move. If the percentage is measured from the wrong base, your first read of that move is wrong, and that is the read you act on. Extended-hours volume is also a small fraction of the regular session, so the print is not a price that size could actually get.
A 30-second test. Take a stock that reported after yesterday's close. Before the open, write down the price and the percentage, then compute the change yourself against the previous regular close. If they disagree, the app is mixing sessions. Then look for a session label in the row. If it is not there, treat every percentage on that screen as approximate.
